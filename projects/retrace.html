Re:Trace

NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026 / In Development

Overview

Re:Trace is an educational storytelling and exploration platform developed for the NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026 challenge, “Abandoned but not Forgotten: Storytelling about NASA's Discarded Equipment on the Moon and Mars.” It focuses on the equipment behind space exploration and the stories that can be overlooked when attention is placed only on spacecraft, missions, and major discoveries.

The platform is being designed to help visitors explore NASA equipment associated with the Moon and Mars through mission context, equipment histories, technical information, scientific contributions, timelines, status classifications, and supporting evidence.

The Problem

Information about space-exploration equipment is spread across mission archives, technical documents, image collections, and historical records. It can be difficult for students and general audiences to understand what individual pieces of equipment did, why they were sent, what they contributed, and what is known about their present status.

Re:Trace addresses this discovery and storytelling challenge by organizing equipment-centred information into a connected, educational experience.

The Approach

The project is organized around two destinations — the Moon and Mars — and a shared equipment-discovery experience. Rather than treating the end of a mission as proof that every associated item was abandoned, the research process distinguishes equipment status from mission status and requires evidence for classifications.

Moon+ Mars→ Equipment Records→ Evidence-led Stories

The content workflow separates screen-specific information requirements, equipment identity management, research, independent verification, approval, and website integration. This helps keep records consistent across different parts of the experience.

Research and Accuracy

Re:Trace follows a NASA-focused research approach, prioritizing official NASA, Jet Propulsion Laboratory, NSSDC, Planetary Data System, and NASA History Office material. Equipment identity, mission association, technical specifications, timelines, status, and location are treated as claims that need supporting evidence.

  • Use official and traceable sources for factual claims.
  • Keep unknown or unsupported values null rather than guessing.
  • Separate equipment status from overall mission status.
  • Distinguish abandoned, inactive, lost, retired, crashed, decommissioned, and operational classifications.
  • Keep verified historical information separate from interpretation and creative storytelling.

My Work and Tools

Re:Trace involves project planning, screen and data-structure design, research coordination, evidence review, and website development. The project workflow uses AI-assisted tools including ChatGPT, Claude, and AWS Quick as part of the planning and development process.

The established website direction uses HTML, CSS, vanilla JavaScript, and JSON-based data, with a lightweight client-side structure.

Specific individual responsibilities and implementation contributions should be updated to match the work completed by the portfolio owner and the project team.

Challenges

  • Finding reliable historical evidence for equipment-level stories.
  • Distinguishing equipment identity and status from the wider mission outcome.
  • Keeping technical details, timelines, and sources consistent across multiple screens.
  • Designing reusable Moon and Mars experiences while keeping their information distinct.
  • Turning complex mission history into accessible educational content without oversimplifying the facts.
